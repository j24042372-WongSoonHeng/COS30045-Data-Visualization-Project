Data Source Notes

Traffic injuries remain the dominant category at 410,884 — over 61% of all transport-related hospitalisations during the decade.

Non-traffic transport (169,963) includes off-road vehicles, farm machinery, and rail incidents.

Methodology

Database: AIHW National Hospital Morbidity Database (NHMD), ICD-10-AM coded records July 2011 – June 2021.

Scope: Principal diagnosis of transport injury; excludes late-effect codes.

Key Findings

Pedestrian stays are longest (8.16 FN vs 9.10 non-Indigenous days) due to severe trauma mechanisms.

Cyclist stays are shortest (~2.9 days both groups), reflecting lower-speed incidents.

Equity Context

First Nations people represent only 4.7% of the population but show a 92.5% admission growth rate vs 15.9% for non-Indigenous over the same decade.

Remoteness is a major driver of longer stays and higher pedestrian proportions.

Remoteness & Regional Factors

Northern Territory & Tasmania: Display longer average stay per injury (5.63 and 5.49 days) largely due to remote transfer distances, higher severity, and post-stabilisation stays.

ACT: Shortest average length of stay (4.08 days) reflecting compact urban road networks and proximity to trauma centres.
